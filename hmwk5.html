Research on Measures of Location & Dispersion

Goal

In this research task, I review the main measures of location (central tendency) and the measures of dispersion that describe variability around the center. Different measures exist to handle skewness, outliers, scales, and ratios in practical applications.

Measures of Location (Central Tendency)

1) Arithmetic Mean

For values x₁,…,xₙ, the arithmetic mean is x̄ = (1/n) ∑ xᵢ. It is the default when data are roughly symmetric and outlier-free. It is not robust: a single extreme value can shift it a lot.

2) Median

Middle value after ordering; robust to skewness and outliers. Often preferred for incomes or heavy-tailed data.

3) Mode

Most frequent value; the only measure that works for nominal/categorical data.

4) Geometric Mean

GM = (∏ xᵢ)^{1/n} (for positive values). Appropriate for multiplicative processes, growth rates, and log-scale data.

5) Harmonic Mean

HM = n / (∑ 1/xᵢ) (positive values). Correct for rates/ratios (e.g., average speed on equal-length segments).

6) Weighted Mean

x̄_w = (∑ wᵢ xᵢ)/(∑ wᵢ). Accounts for importance/precision/sample size of observations.

7) Trimmed / Winsorized Mean

Trimmed mean drops a small fraction of extremes; Winsorized caps them. A compromise between efficiency (mean) and robustness (median).

Measures of Dispersion (Variability)

  • Range: max − min; simple, but highly sensitive to outliers.
  • Variance (σ²): average squared deviation from the mean (squared units).
  • Standard deviation (σ): √variance; distance from mean in original units.
  • Interquartile range (IQR): Q3 − Q1; robust to outliers.
  • Mean absolute deviation (MAD): average of absolute deviations; interpretable, robust alternative.

Which to use and when

  • Symmetric & no outliers → mean & standard deviation.
  • Skewed / outliers → median & IQR (or MAD).
  • Growth & ratios → geometric or harmonic mean (+ dispersion on log scale if needed).
