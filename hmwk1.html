What is Statistics, and why can it be useful for Cybersecurity?

Definition (brief)

Statistics studies how to collect, summarize, analyze and infer from data under uncertainty. It provides tools (descriptive & inferential) to turn raw data into reliable insight and decisions.

Why it matters for Cybersecurity

  • Anomaly detection: model “normal” traffic (distributions, baselines) and flag outliers for possible intrusions.
  • Threat scoring: estimate probabilities (e.g., Bayes) and false-positive/false-negative trade-offs.
  • Monitoring efficacy: hypothesis tests/intervals to evaluate if a control actually reduces incidents.
  • Incident forensics: summarize logs at scale (sampling, aggregation) to reconstruct events.
  • Risk models: quantify frequency/severity; fit distributions; simulate scenarios.

Mini example

Given daily failed-login counts, build a baseline (mean ± k·σ). Days beyond the threshold trigger review. Tune k to balance sensitivity vs. noise.
