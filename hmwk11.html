HMWK 11 — Brownian Motion & Stochastic Differential Equations

From Random Walks to Brownian Motion (and beyond)

In Homework 7 I simulated a discrete random walk with steps \(+1\) or \(-1\). In this assignment I move to the continuous-time limit and simulate a Wiener process (standard Brownian motion) using the Euler–Maruyama method for stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Optionally, I generalize the simulator to handle more general SDEs of the form \[ dX_t = \mu(X_t,t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t. \]

1) Mathematical Setup

1.1 Wiener process as a limit of random walks

A standard Wiener process \(W_t\) (Brownian motion) is a continuous-time stochastic process satisfying:

  • \(W_0 = 0\).
  • Independent increments.
  • \(W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)\) for \(0 \le s < t\).
  • Paths are continuous (but nowhere differentiable).

Intuitively, we can obtain \(W_t\) as the limit of a symmetric random walk with time step \(\Delta t\) and step size proportional to \(\sqrt{\Delta t}\).

1.2 Euler–Maruyama for SDEs

Consider a general SDE:

dXt = μ(Xt, t) dt + σ(Xt, t) dWt.

On a discrete grid \(t_0, t_1, \dots, t_n\) with uniform step \(\Delta t = T/n\), the Euler–Maruyama approximation is:

Xk+1 = Xk + μ(Xk, tk) Δt + σ(Xk, tk) √(Δt) Zk,

where \(Z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\) are independent standard normals.

A pure Wiener process corresponds to \(\mu(x,t) \equiv 0\), \(\sigma(x,t) \equiv 1\), so

Wk+1 = Wk + √(Δt) Zk.

1.3 Generating Normal Random Variables (Box–Muller)

The simulator needs standard normal variables \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\). I generate them from two independent Uniform(0,1) variables \(U_1, U_2\) using the Box–Muller transform:

Z = √(−2 ln U1) · cos(2π U2).

In JavaScript, I approximate Uniform(0,1) by Math.random().

4) Discussion

4.1 Connection to Homework 7 (Random Walk)

In Homework 7, the random walk evolved with discrete steps \(+1\) and \(-1\). Here I replace those fixed steps with Gaussian increments of size √(Δt) · Z. As Δt → 0, the resulting process converges (in distribution) to a Wiener process. Conceptually, this homework is the continuous-time counterpart of the random walk model.

4.2 Why Euler–Maruyama?

The Euler–Maruyama method is the stochastic analogue of the standard Euler method for ordinary differential equations. It is simple to implement and, for sufficiently small Δt, provides a good approximation of the path distribution of the underlying SDE. More sophisticated schemes (Milstein, higher-order methods) exist, but Euler–Maruyama is a natural first step.
